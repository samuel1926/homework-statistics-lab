HOMEWORK 01 / MATHEMATICAL DERIVATION

Welford's recurrence.
From Knuth to variance.

We need a numerically stable, one-pass way to update both the mean and the sum of squared deviations when a new observation arrives — without storing all past observations.

STEP 01 / KNUTH'S MEAN

Update the running mean

For the first k observations, write the new mean in terms of the previous mean and the newest value xk.

μk = [(k − 1)μk−1 + xk] / k
μk = μk−1 + δk / k
δk = xk − μk−1

The correction δk/k decreases as more observations are collected.

STEP 02 / DEFINE SUM OF SQUARES

Track the centered SS

The quantity M2,k (also written SSk) is the sum of squared differences from the current sample mean.

M2,k = Σi=1…k(xi − μk)2
M2,1 = 0

The mean changes after every new observation, so simply adding (xk − old mean)2 would be incorrect.

STEP 03 / DERIVE THE RECURRENCE

Expand the old deviations around the new mean.

01

For i < k, write xi − μk = (xi − μk−1) + (μk−1 − μk).

02

Expand the square and sum. The cross term vanishes because Σi=1…k−1(xi − μk−1) = 0.

03

Since μk − μk−1 = δk/k, and xk − μk = (k−1)δk/k, the two extra squared terms simplify.

M2,k = M2,k−1 + (k−1)(μk−1 − μk)2 + (xk − μk)2
= M2,k−1 + [(k−1)/k²]δk2 + [(k−1)²/k²]δk2
WELFORD'S SUM-OF-SQUARES RECURRENCE
M2,k = M2,k−1 + [(k−1)/k] δk2
= M2,k−1 + δk(xk − μk)
UPDATED MEANμk = μk−1 + δk/k
UNBIASED SAMPLE VARIANCEsk2 = M2,k / (k − 1)k ≥ 2
ONLINE VARIANCE RECURRENCEsk2 = [(k−2)/(k−1)]sk−12 + δk(xk − μk)/(k−1)k ≥ 3
i

Two denominators, two meanings. Divide SS by k − 1 for the unbiased sample variance; divide by k for the descriptive population variance of the observed values. At k = 1, the sample variance is undefined.

HOMEWORK 01 / IMPLEMENTATION

Three lines do the work.

After incrementing the sample counter, each new value updates both the mean and the centered sum of squares using only three statements.

WELFORD.JSVanilla JavaScript · O(n) time / O(1) extra space
function welford(values) {
  let n = 0, mean = 0, M2 = 0;

  for (const x of values) {
    n++;
    const delta = x - mean;       // 1. distance from old mean
    mean += delta / n;            // 2. Knuth recurrence
    M2 += delta * (x - mean);     // 3. Welford recurrence
  }

  return {
    mean,
    SS: M2,
    sampleVariance: n > 1 ? M2 / (n - 1) : NaN,
    populationVariance: n > 0 ? M2 / n : NaN
  };
}

console.log(welford([2, 8, 4, 10, 6]));
// { mean: 6, SS: 40, sampleVariance: 10, populationVariance: 8 }

Many attackers.
One known distribution.

Each of the m hackers attempts n independent systems. Every attempt yields an integer score from 0 to 10. We estimate the mean and unbiased sample variance separately for every hacker, then average the estimates across hackers.

FIXED DISCRETE PROBABILITY MODEL

Uniform scores on {0, 1, …, 10}

All 11 outcomes have equal probability P(X = k) = 1/11, for k = 0, …, 10. We hold this distribution fixed in every experiment.

One trajectory, two estimators.

x̄a,n = (1/n)Σi=1…nXa,i
sa,n2 = [1/(n−1)]Σ(Xa,i − x̄a,n)2

Welford computes both statistics during the n draws, without revisiting the data.

ACROSS HACKERS / MONTE CARLO

Average the m estimates.

AvgMean = (1/m)Σa=1…mx̄a,n
AvgVariance = (1/m)Σa=1…msa,n2

For IID scores and n ≥ 2, both estimators are unbiased: their expected values are 5 and 10.

WHY THE MEAN CONCENTRATESVar(x̄n) = σ²/n = 10/n

By independence, sample means become less variable at the exact 1/n variance rate. Their standard deviation shrinks as 1/√n.

WHY THE VARIANCE CONCENTRATESVar(s²n) = [78 + 200/(n−1)] / n

For this discrete uniform distribution (fourth central moment = 178), the unbiased sample variance also becomes less variable as n increases.

CONCLUSIONS / INFERENTIAL STATISTICS

From samples to population claims.

These experiments establish why sample statistics can serve as estimators of unknown parameters — and why increasing the sample size makes individual estimates more trustworthy.

01 / UNBIASEDNESS

Correct in expectation.

E[x̄] = μ = 5 and E[s²] = σ² = 10 for every n ≥ 2 under the IID model. A finite Monte Carlo average is close to, not necessarily equal to, its target.

02 / CONSISTENCY

More data, tighter estimates.

Increasing n reduces the spread of individual sampling means and variances. Both estimators converge to their population targets under these assumptions.

03 / TWO DIFFERENT SAMPLE SIZES

n and m have different jobs.

n is observations per hacker and controls each estimate's precision. m is the number of independent replicated estimates and improves the precision of the Monte Carlo averages.

04 / INFERENCE

Quantifying uncertainty.

Sampling distributions underpin standard errors, confidence intervals and hypothesis tests. Welford lets us calculate the needed estimates online, stably and efficiently.

THE FULL ANSWER / LESSON 04

Unbiasedness describes the average across repetitions. Consistency describes what happens to a single estimator as n grows.

The two ideas are related, but they are not the same; our experiment makes both visible.
